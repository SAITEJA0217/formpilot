## Information sheet

**Study** Reviewing automated suggestions when filling web forms
**Investigator** [name, affiliation, email]
**Ethics approval** [body, reference number, date]

### What this is

We are studying how people fill in online application forms, and whether a browser tool that suggests
answers makes it easier or harder. You will fill in several forms, some with the tool and some without,
and we will record how long each takes and what you enter.

### Why you

You have completed an online application form in the last year, so the forms will be familiar to you.
We are not testing you. We are testing the tool, and a form that is hard to fill in is a finding about
the tool, not about you.

### What you will do

About 50 minutes:

- read and sign this form
- read a short briefing and a made-up personal profile on paper
- fill in one practice form
- fill in four forms, two with the tool and two without
- answer some questions, including one where you can say whatever you like

### You will not use your own personal details

You will be given a **made-up profile** — a fictional person's name, address, employer and so on — and
asked to fill the forms in as that person. Please do not enter any of your own details at any point.

The forms are on the computer in front of you and are not connected to anything. Nothing you type is
sent to any company or website. If you do enter something of your own by accident, tell us and we will
delete that form's data.

### The tool is sometimes wrong

The tool suggests answers, and some of its suggestions are wrong. You decide whether to accept, change
or reject each one. We are interested in exactly that, so please treat its suggestions as suggestions.
There is no penalty for rejecting all of them.

### What we record

- how long each form takes
- what ends up in each field
- for each suggestion, whether you accepted, changed or rejected it
- your questionnaire answers

We do **not** record your name, your screen, your face, your voice, or anything you type outside the
forms. Your data is stored under a participant code, and the sheet linking codes to names is destroyed
once data collection ends.

### You can stop

You can stop at any time, for any reason, without explaining, and without any consequence. If you stop
we will ask whether to keep the forms you have already completed or delete them, and either answer is
fine. You can also withdraw afterwards by emailing [address] and quoting your participant code, up to
[date], after which the data is aggregated and individual records can no longer be picked out.

### Who wrote the tool

The tool being studied was written by the investigator. That is a conflict of interest and we are
telling you because you should know it. It is also why the scoring is done by a script that is not told
which condition produced which form.

### Questions

[investigator email]. For a concern you would rather not raise with the investigator: [independent
contact].
